# waste-biomethane-ccs-siting

Code and figure source data for the manuscript **“Waste biomethane–CCS enables cost-effective mitigation and geological CO<sub>2</sub> storage in China”**.

**Authors:** Leichang Cao, Chenlin Wei, Jieni Wang, Haodong Hou, Huilan Hou, Gang Luo, Angelidaki Irini, Daniel C.W. Tsang, Yifei Sun, Shicheng Zhang, James H. Clark 


## Abstract
Scalable carbon dioxide removal is needed to meet climate goals, yet the deployable potential of waste-derived bioenergy with carbon capture and storage remains poorly constrained by sustainability and siting limitations. Here we map crop residues, livestock manure and food waste across China, and combine spatial siting, life-cycle assessment and techno-economic analysis to evaluate waste biomethane–CCS. We estimate a theoretical biomethane potential of 99.5 billion m<sup>3</sup> yr<sup>−1</sup>. A portfolio of 500 plants using 44% of this resource delivers 68.90 MtCO<sub>2</sub>eq yr<sup>−1</sup> of total mitigation, including substitution benefits from biomethane and digestate, at an average mitigation cost of −US$5.74 tCO<sub>2</sub>eq<sup>−1</sup>; this corresponds to 51.47 MtCO<sub>2</sub> yr<sup>−1</sup> of gross geological CO<sub>2</sub> storage. Cost-effective opportunities cluster in eastern Sichuan, Northwest China, and parts of East and Central South China. These results show that waste biomethane–CCS could provide a cost-effective, land-sparing climate solution in China, while underscoring the need for spatially targeted deployment to translate resource availability into realized mitigation.

## Repository contents
- `scripts/` — ArcGIS Pro (Python window) siting script (sliding-window hotspot selection with iterative feedstock deduction).
- `data/` — Figure source data and key input/summary tables (CSV).
