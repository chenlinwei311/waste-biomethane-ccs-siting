# waste-biomethane-ccs-siting

Code and figure source data for the manuscript **“A transferable framework for distributed BECCS offers prioritized deployability across geographies and policy contexts”**.

**Authors:** Leichang Cao, Chenlin Wei, Jieni Wang, Haodong Hou, Huilan Hou, Gang Luo, Angelidaki Irini, Daniel C.W. Tsang, Yifei Sun, Shicheng Zhang, James H. Clark 


## Abstract
Deploying distributed bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS) requires reconciling spatially heterogeneous biomass resources, sustainability constraints, and the need to distinguish durable geological CO<sub>2</sub> storage from total mitigation. Here, we develop a deployability-constrained framework that integrates high-resolution resource mapping, iterative siting under feedstock competition, process-based life-cycle assessment, and plant-level techno-economic analysis under uncertainty, and we demonstrate it using waste-based biomethane–CCS in China as a worked example. China’s waste biomass has a theoretical biomethane potential of 99.5 billion m<sup>3</sup> yr<sup>−1</sup>. An optimized portfolio of 500 plants utilizing 44% of this potential delivers total mitigation (E<sub>Mit</sub>) of 68.90 MtCO<sub>2</sub>eq yr<sup>−1</sup> (95% CI 61.11–83.49), while storing 51.47 MtCO<sub>2</sub> yr<sup>−1</sup> as durable geological CO<sub>2</sub> (E<sub>CDR</sub>), at an average mitigation cost of −US$5.74 tCO<sub>2</sub>eq<sup>−1</sup>. Cost-effective opportunities cluster in eastern Sichuan, Northwest China, and parts of East and Central South China. A targeted incentive of US$30 tCO<sub>2</sub>eq<sup>−1</sup> unlocks 65.63 MtCO<sub>2</sub>eq yr<sup>−1</sup> of E<sub>Mit</sub>. More broadly, the framework highlights three transferable principles for distributed BECCS: deployability depends more on spatial clustering than on resource totals alone, durable geological storage must be distinguished from total mitigation, and carbon-price incentives must be complemented by methane control and governance safeguards. Together, it offers a transferable basis for prioritizing distributed BECCS across geographies and policy contexts.

## Repository contents
- `scripts/` — ArcGIS Pro (Python window) siting script (sliding-window hotspot selection with iterative feedstock deduction).
- `data/` — Figure source data and key input/summary tables (CSV).
